# Current Manual Tea Collection Workflow

## Overview

The current tea collection process used by the client is mostly manual and depends on physical route books, farmer record books, and manual office data entry. Tea collectors travel through assigned routes to collect tea leaves from farmers and record collection details manually. At the end of the day, office staff re-enter all collection data into the existing desktop-based accounting system.

This workflow analysis was prepared to understand the existing operational process and identify system limitations before designing the TeaRoutePay solution.

---

## Stakeholders / Users Involved

### Farmer / Supplier
- Provides tea leaves to the collector
- Receives monthly payments
- Maintains personal tea collection records

### Tea Collector
- Travels through assigned collection routes
- Measures tea leaf weight
- Records collection details manually
- Delivers collection information to the office

### Office Staff / Owner
- Re-enters daily collection records into the desktop system
- Handles payment calculations
- Manages deductions and accounting records
- Generates payment receipts

---

## Existing Manual Workflow

### Step 1 – Daily Route Preparation
At the beginning of the day, the tea collector receives the assigned route details and prepares the lorry route book used for recording daily tea collection activities.

### Step 2 – Farmer Tea Leaf Handover
Farmers provide tea leaves to the tea collector during route visits. Each farmer maintains an their own handwritten collection book issued by the office, and the collector updates this book during collection activities.

### Step 3 – Tea Weight Measurement
The tea collector manually measures the tea leaf weight using a weighing scale.

### Step 4 – Manual Record Entry
The collector manually records collection information in the route book and updates the farmer’s hardwritten collection record book, including:
- Farmer name or identification
- Tea leaf weight
- Date and collection details
- Fertilizer or deduction notes if required

### Step 5 – Route Completion
The collector travels through the assigned route and collects tea leaves from each farmer location during the daily collection period.

### Step 6 – End-of-Day Office Submission
After completing the route, the tea collector returns to the office and submits the route book containing the daily collection records.

### Step 7 – Manual Office Data Entry
Office staff manually enter all route collection records into the existing desktop accounting system.

### Step 8 – Monthly Payment Processing
The office calculates:
- Tea payments
- Loan deductions
- Fertilizer deductions
- Tea-for-personal-use deductions

Monthly receipts are then generated for farmers.

## Existing Records and Documents Used

The current workflow uses several manual and semi-digital records:

- Manual farmer collection books
- Lorry route books
- Existing desktop accounting system
- Printed payment receipts
- Bank payment letters

---

## Record Handling Process

The current process uses both route books and official farmer collection books as operational and backup records.

1. Tea collection details are recorded in the collector’s route book.
2. The same collection details are updated in the farmer’s official collection book.
3. Office staff enter collection records into the desktop accounting system for payment processing and reporting.
4. Physical books are maintained for verification and backup purposes.

---

## Manual Pain Points

1. End-of-day office data entry increases workload and processing time.
2. Handwritten records may become unclear, damaged, or lost.
3. Human errors can occur during manual calculations and data entry.
4. The current desktop system does not support mobile field collection.
5. Farmer identification is fully manual.
6. Reporting and record tracking are time-consuming.
7. The system does not support digital field data capture or real-time synchronization.

---

## Human Error Risk Areas

Human errors may occur in the following situations:

- Incorrect tea weight recording
- Duplicate or missing entries
- Calculation mistakes during payment processing
- Misreading handwritten records
- Incorrect farmer identification
- Data entry mistakes during office record input

---

## Improvement Opportunities

Several improvements can be introduced through the TeaRoutePay system:

1. Introduce QR-based farmer identification.
2. Use a mobile application for route collection activities.
3. Support offline-first data collection for rural areas with poor internet connectivity.
4. Reduce duplicate office data entry through direct synchronization.
5. Provide local and cloud synchronization support.
6. Generate digital payment receipts automatically.
7. Send SMS confirmations after payment processing.
8. Improve reporting and tracking capabilities.
9. Improve overall payment accuracy and operational efficiency.

---

## Conclusion

The current tea collection workflow heavily depends on manual record keeping and repeated data entry processes. Although the existing system supports accounting operations, the collection workflow remains inefficient and prone to human error. The TeaRoutePay solution aims to improve efficiency, reduce manual workload, and modernize tea collection and payment management using an offline-first digital approach.
